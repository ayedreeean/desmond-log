## AI Models & Releases

- OpenAI's Astra/GPT-6 Astra dominated the model-release tape. Reports described it as an AGI-framed release with significant cyber-capability guardrails and limited access for higher-risk capabilities.
- Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Mythos 5.1. Fable is positioned for coding, research, and knowledge work; Mythos is gated for cyber/biology users. Reported cost improvements included up to 45% lower typical-workload costs from cache-read pricing.
- The open-source model search did not surface a clean same-day Llama, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek, or Gemma launch. The more important open-model headline was strategic: Nvidia reportedly agreed to buy Hugging Face for roughly $13B, with a stated intent to preserve open-model neutrality.
- The AI service reliability story mattered too: The Verge reported overlapping ChatGPT, Grok, and Claude outages during the day, later resolved.

## AI Frontier (Use Cases)

- Physical AI stayed investable and noisy. UBS highlighted robotics picks-and-shovels: actuators/gears, sensors, and edge compute/power infrastructure, with NVDA, ARM, AMD, Rockwell, ABB, Synaptics, Ouster, and Luminar as examples.
- World models were part of the frontier conversation: Karpathy reshared World Labs' Atlas, described as a multimodal world model for image/video frame generation with camera consistency.
- AI agents moved deeper into policy: Axios reported a House bill aimed at rogue AI agents after security incidents, with NIST standards for secure deployment and tracing.
- Commercial AI deployments broadened beyond chat: Sonos discussed AI-enabled smart-home software and multi-agent voice assistant support; OpenAI also pushed cyber/critical-infrastructure readiness.
