**Performance:** Up $2,197 in a single session, jumping from +14.90% to +17.10%. That's our best day in weeks and it came exactly when you'd expect — the snapback after two days of semiconductor carnage. Every position contributed: TXN led the charge at +5.35%, CRWD added +6.8%, DDOG climbed another 2.9%, and NVDA reclaimed $200 with a +2.2% bounce. This is what happens when you don't panic-sell into a sector rotation.

**Market Observations:** Today was the mirror image of the last two sessions. SMH ripped +6.75%, nearly erasing the entire Broadcom-triggered selloff in one move. AMD led the charge at +7.97%. The catalysts were real: CPI came in softer than feared, and Trump announced a diplomatic breakthrough with Iran — the single biggest macro overhang on this tape. If the Iran discount was worth 10% on SPX (as some estimates suggested), today was the market starting to price that out.

TXN at $297 is knocking on the door of Wells Fargo's $300 price target from yesterday. Analog semis continue to behave like a different asset class than the digital/AI chip names — less volatile down, and still participating on the upside.

DDOG at $234 is now an 84% winner and approaching a legitimate double from our $127 entry. The observability thesis was never about the semiconductor cycle — it was about every company needing to monitor increasingly complex AI infrastructure. That secular trend doesn't care about Broadcom's guidance.

**Thesis Updates:** No position changes. The playbook remains the same: hold winners, keep the cash pile ready.

- **NVDA (+7.8%):** Survived the $200 test and bounced. The AI capex story isn't dead — it just got a stress test. Next catalyst is the next earnings cycle.
- **TXN (+31.1%):** Best fundamental story in the book right now. Analog recovery is real, and this name quietly outperforms while everyone argues about NVDA.
- **DDOG (+84.0%):** The crown jewel. Approaching a double. No reason to touch this — enterprise observability spend is accelerating, not decelerating.
- **CRWD (+66.3%):** Iran tensions arguably made the cybersecurity thesis stronger. Even as geopolitical risk cools, the cybersecurity spend ratchet only turns one direction.

63% cash remains intentional. We just watched $1.3T evaporate from semiconductors and come roaring back in 24 hours. That kind of volatility rewards patience and punishes leverage. The cash is optionality — and today proved the positions we do hold are the right ones.
