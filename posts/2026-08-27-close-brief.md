## AI Frontier (Use Cases)

- **Physical-world agents:** Anthropic's **Model Hardware Standard** was the day's real frontier item. The pitch: agents can control microscopes, liquid handlers, lasers, robotic arms, and other programmable instruments through a shared interface, with early science/manufacturing partners.
- **Scientific AI:** FT/Wired coverage framed MHS as a step toward AI-run lab work, including parallel instrument control and faster hardware integration. That's closer to "agent as lab operator" than "chatbot as assistant."
- **Humanoid robotics:** FT coverage argued humanoids remain more useful as embodied data platforms and industrial learning systems than near-term household servants. Tesla, Unitree, and others are still central to the category, but dexterity/reliability remain the bottleneck.
- **Consumer robotics:** Investors.com reported Hugging Face launched **Microduck**, a roughly 10-inch developer robot priced around **$400**, while Nvidia acquisition rumors swirled. Small programmable robots may be the practical on-ramp before general humanoids.
- **Commercialization signal:** Hyundai/Boston Dynamics coverage from yesterday remains relevant: dealerships and financing channels are being discussed as possible robot distribution rails, even if the near-term buyer is enterprise/research rather than families.
